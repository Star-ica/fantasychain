1. Executive Summary

FantasyChain is a fully onchain fantasy sports protocol deployed on BOT Chain (EVM chain ID 677). It enables any organizer to create a skill-based fantasy sports competition, collect entry fees, and automatically distribute prizes to top-ranked participants — with every critical step recorded and verifiable on a public blockchain.

Traditional fantasy sports platforms are opaque: prize pools are held in centralized wallets, scoring is performed by proprietary algorithms, and result disputes have no binding on-chain mechanism. FantasyChain eliminates these trust dependencies. Prize funds are locked in a smart contract escrow from the moment a competition is created. Scores are submitted by a designated oracle and stored on-chain. Prize distribution follows deterministic rules encoded in the contracts. Winners claim their prizes directly from the escrow without relying on any platform intermediary.

Key properties of the protocol:

  • Non-custodial escrow. Prize pools are held in PrizeEscrow, not in any operator wallet. Neither the organizer nor the platform can redirect funds once a competition finalizes.
  • Transparent scoring. All team selections and oracle-submitted scores are stored on-chain. Any third party can independently verify the final rankings.
  • Composable architecture. Six specialized contracts — each owning a single responsibility — can be upgraded or extended independently without breaking the broader system.
  • Multi-sport support. Football, basketball, cricket, baseball, soccer, tennis, and a generic "other" category are natively supported through the SportType enum.
  • Dual currency. Competitions accept either the native BOT token or any ERC-20 (default: USDT) for entry fees and prize pools.

The protocol is designed to serve three primary users: organizers who fund and configure competitions; participants who build fantasy teams and pay entry fees; and oracles who submit real-world athletic performance data to the chain.

2. Protocol Architecture

2.1 System Overview

FantasyChain is composed of six Solidity contracts. Each contract has a single primary responsibility and communicates with its peers through typed interfaces rather than direct inheritance. All contracts inherit OpenZeppelin's Ownable and Pausable primitives; contracts that handle value transfers also inherit ReentrancyGuard.

Contract Responsibility Holds Funds?
PlayerRegistry Global registry of real-world athletes eligible for selection No
CompetitionFactory Creates competitions; holds organizer's initial prize pool contribution until finalization Yes (organizer prize pool)
TeamManager Accepts team submissions; validates player eligibility and entry fees Transiently (entry fee routing)
ScoringManager Accepts and stores oracle-submitted scores per team per competition No
PrizeEscrow Locks prize pool; computes winner allocations; releases funds to ClaimManager Yes (prize funds)
ClaimManager Allows winners to pull their prize allocations; handles ERC-20 and native claims Yes (pending claims)

The data flow for a full competition lifecycle is as follows:

  1. Organizer calls CompetitionFactory.createCompetition(), locking the prize pool on-chain.
  2. Registrar adds eligible athletes via PlayerRegistry.batchRegisterPlayers().
  3. Participants call TeamManager.createTeam() before the submission deadline, paying entry fees.
  4. After the real-world event concludes, the oracle submits scores via ScoringManager.submitScores() and transitions the competition to SCORING status.
  5. Oracle or owner calls CompetitionFactory.updateStatus(FINALIZED).
  6. Prize allocations are computed by PrizeEscrow based on final rankings.
  7. Winners call ClaimManager.claim() to withdraw their prizes.

3. Smart Contract Reference

3.1 PlayerRegistry

Purpose: Maintains a global, immutable-by-default registry of real-world athletes. Each player is assigned a globally unique sequential ID. Players are associated with one or more competitions via a separate mapping, allowing the same athlete to participate across multiple events.

Key Data Structures

  • Player struct — stores id, name, realTeam, position (enum covering football through general), sport (uint8), status (Active / Injured / Suspended / Retired), registeredAt, and exists.
  • competitionPlayers[competitionId] — array of player IDs assigned to each competition.
  • playerIndexInComp[competitionId][playerId] — O(1) lookup and O(1) removal via swap-and-pop.

3.2 CompetitionFactory

Purpose: The entry point for organizers. Manages the full lifecycle of a competition from creation through cancellation or finalization. Holds the organizer's initial prize pool until the competition concludes or is cancelled.

Competition Lifecycle States

OPEN → CLOSED → SCORING → DISPUTED → FINALIZED → CANCELLED

Status transitions are permitted by the organizer, the competition's oracle, or the contract owner. This flexibility allows the oracle to move a competition into SCORING once results are ready, the organizer to flag a DISPUTED state, and the owner to finalize or cancel in edge cases.

3.3 TeamManager

Purpose: Accepts team submissions from participants. Enforces one team per wallet per competition, validates that all selected players are registered in the target competition, and routes entry fees to the platform fee recipient and prize pool.

Key Functions

Entry Fee Flow

When a participant submits a team with an entry fee, TeamManager splits the payment:

  • Platform fee (entryFee × platformFeeBps / 10000) is forwarded immediately to feeRecipient.
  • The remainder (poolContribution) is forwarded to PrizeEscrow or held by TeamManager pending escrow integration.
  • Any overpayment beyond entryFee is refunded to the sender in the same transaction.

3.4 ScoringManager

Purpose: Accepts and stores real-world performance scores submitted by the designated oracle after a competition's real-world event concludes. Maintains per-team, per-competition score records and provides ranking data to PrizeEscrow.

Score Submission Model

Scores are submitted as an array of (teamId, score) pairs for a given competition. The oracle must be the address recorded on the competition in CompetitionFactory. Scores are accepted only while the competition is in SCORING status. Once all scores are submitted, the oracle transitions the competition to FINALIZED.

Key Functions

Purpose: The financial heart of the protocol. Locks prize pool funds from CompetitionFactory and entry-fee contributions from TeamManager. 4. Scoring Engine & Oracle Model

4.1 On-Chain vs. Off-Chain Scoring

FantasyChain separates scoring logic from scoring execution. The rules — which actions earn how many points — are defined off-chain in a JSON document whose keccak256 hash is stored in the competition record as scoringRulesHash. Any participant can independently verify that the oracle applied the rules faithfully by hashing the published JSON and comparing it to the on-chain value.

The results — the computed score for every team — are written on-chain by the oracle via ScoringManager.submitScores(). This creates a permanent, tamper-proof record of every team's performance.

4.2 Oracle Authorization

Each competition specifies an oracle address at creation time. This address is the sole party permitted to submit scores to ScoringManager for that competition. The oracle may also transition the competition to SCORING and subsequently FINALIZED status.

Globally whitelisted oracle addresses are tracked in CompetitionFactory.authorizedOracles. The contract owner can authorize or revoke oracle status at any time. For high-value competitions, a hardware-secured oracle or a multisig is strongly recommended.

4.3 Scoring Rule Schema

The off-chain scoring JSON follows this schema (example for soccer):

{
  "sport": "SOCCER",
  "version": "1.0",
  "actions": [
    { "event": "goal",        "points": 6,  "positions": ["FORWARD","MIDFIELDER"] },
    { "event": "goal",        "points": 10, "positions": ["DEFENDER","GOALKEEPER"] },
    { "event": "assist",      "points": 3,  "positions": ["*"] },
    { "event": "clean_sheet", "points": 4,  "positions": ["GOALKEEPER","DEFENDER"] },
    { "event": "yellow_card", "points": -1, "positions": ["*"] },
    { "event": "red_card",    "points": -3, "positions": ["*"] },
    { "event": "appearance",  "points": 2,  "positions": ["*"] }
  ]
}

The hash of this document is computed as keccak256(abi.encodePacked(jsonString)) and stored in Competition.scoringRulesHash at creation time.

4.4 Score Finality

Scores submitted via submitScores() are final once the competition transitions to FINALIZED. If a score submission error is discovered before finalization, the oracle may resubmit corrected scores while the competition remains in SCORING status. After finalization, scores are immutable.

5. Prize Distribution Mechanics

5.1 Prize Pool Composition

The total prize pool for a competition is the sum of:

  • Organizer contribution — the msg.value (or USDT amount) locked when createCompetition() is called.
  • Entry fee contributions — the net entry fees (after platform fee deduction) forwarded to PrizeEscrow from TeamManager as participants register teams.

5.2 Default Distribution Split

When finalizeAndAllocate() is called, PrizeEscrow queries ScoringManager for the top three teams and divides the total prize pool as follows:

PlaceShareExample (1,000 BOT pool)
1st Place50%500 BOT
2nd Place30%300 BOT
3rd Place20%200 BOT

The organizer may configure a custom split at competition creation time by passing a prizeDistribution array summing to 10,000 basis points. Custom distributions must be validated on-chain before the competition opens.

5.3 Fewer Than Three Teams

If fewer than three teams entered a competition:

  • With one team: the single participant receives 100% of the prize pool.
  • With two teams: the winner receives 65%; the runner-up receives 35% (proportional rescaling of the top two bands).

These edge-case distributions are computed dynamically by PrizeEscrow at finalization.

5.4 Unclaimed Prizes

Prize allocations that remain unclaimed after a configurable claimWindow (default: 90 days from finalization) may be swept by the contract owner to a designated charity or treasury address. This prevents prize funds from being permanently locked due to abandoned wallets. The claim window and sweep destination are set at PrizeEscrow deployment.

5.5 Platform Fee

The platform fee is deducted from each entry fee payment at the time of team submission, before any funds reach PrizeEscrow. The platform fee does not reduce the organizer's initial prize pool contribution — it applies only to participant entry fees. See Section 9 for full fee mechanics.

6. Result Verification & Dispute Process

6.1 Verification by Any Party

Because all team selections and oracle-submitted scores are stored on-chain, any party can verify the correctness of a competition result without requesting data from the platform:

  1. Retrieve all team player selections from TeamManager.getCompetitionTeamDetails().
  2. Retrieve all submitted scores from ScoringManager.getRankings().
  3. Download the scoring rules JSON from the off-chain URI published by the organizer and verify its hash matches Competition.scoringRulesHash.
  4. Independently compute each team's score from the rules JSON and the publicly available real-world event data (official sports data APIs, league websites, etc.).
  5. Compare independently computed scores to the oracle-submitted scores on-chain.

Any discrepancy discovered in this process constitutes grounds for a formal dispute.

6.2 Dispute Initiation

Any participant may initiate a dispute by calling CompetitionFactory.updateStatus(competitionId, DISPUTED). Only the organizer, oracle, or owner can call this function. In practice, a participant who discovers a scoring error should contact the organizer, who then transitions the competition to DISPUTED.

Note: A competition in DISPUTED status halts all prize claims via ClaimManager until the dispute is resolved. The contract owner can override the status to either FINALIZED (accepting corrected scores) or CANCELLED (triggering refunds).

6.3 Dispute Resolution

OutcomeActionResult
Scores confirmed correctOwner / Oracle transitions to FINALIZEDOriginal prize allocations stand; claims re-open.
Scores correctedOracle resubmits corrected scores while in DISPUTED; then transitions to FINALIZEDNew allocations computed; claims open with corrected amounts.
Competition invalidOwner transitions to CANCELLEDPrizeEscrow releases all funds back to organizer; participant entry fees refunded pro-rata.

6.4 On-Chain Arbitration (Future)

The current release relies on the contract owner as the final arbiter. A future protocol upgrade will integrate an on-chain arbitration module where a committee of staked validators can be called upon to adjudicate disputes. Each validator reviews the evidence and casts a weighted vote. The majority verdict updates the competition's final scores on-chain without requiring off-chain coordination.

7. Compliance & Skill-Based Gaming

7.1 Fantasy Sports as a Skill-Based Activity

Fantasy sports competitions are widely recognized in numerous jurisdictions as skill-based games, legally distinct from gambling. This distinction rests on three pillars:

  • Outcome determined by participant skill. The result depends predominantly on the participant's knowledge of athletes, statistics, and strategic team-building decisions — not on a random number generator or house-controlled outcome.
  • Real-world event basis. Scores derive from actual athletic performance in real sporting events, not from simulated or generated results.
  • No house edge against participants. The platform collects a fixed service fee from entry fees; it does not take positions against participants or benefit from any particular outcome.

FantasyChain's architecture reinforces all three pillars. Team selections are publicly stored on-chain before the sporting event occurs (preventing retroactive adjustment). Scoring rules are hashed into the competition record before entries open (preventing post-hoc rule changes). The platform fee is fixed and transparent (preventing hidden house advantages).

7.2 Jurisdictional Considerations

The legal status of fantasy sports varies by jurisdiction. Operators deploying FantasyChain are solely responsible for assessing and complying with applicable laws in jurisdictions where their users reside. The protocol itself is neutral infrastructure; it does not enforce geographic restrictions. Operators may implement jurisdiction-based access controls at the frontend layer.

Key jurisdictions where fantasy sports are expressly permitted as skill games include the United States (with certain state-level exclusions), the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, and India. Operators should obtain independent legal counsel before launching in any jurisdiction.

7.3 KYC / AML Integration

The FantasyChain smart contracts do not enforce KYC/AML procedures — these are matters for the operator layer. Operators may integrate identity verification at the frontend or implement an allowlist controlled by an admin role before participants are permitted to call TeamManager.createTeam().

7.4 Minor Protection

Operators must implement age verification at the application layer to prevent participation by minors in jurisdictions where such restrictions apply. The smart contracts do not enforce age requirements.

8. Security Model

8.1 Reentrancy Protection

All contracts that execute external calls (ether or ERC-20 transfers) inherit OpenZeppelin's ReentrancyGuard and apply the nonReentrant modifier to every external function that moves funds. The contracts additionally follow the checks-effects-interactions pattern: state variables are updated before any external call is made.

8.2 Access Control

Role-based access control is enforced across all contracts:

RolePermissionsMechanism
OwnerAdmin functions, pause/unpause, emergency withdrawal, arbitration overrideOZ Ownable
RegistrarRegister players, update player statusMapping in PlayerRegistry
OracleSubmit scores, advance competition statusPer-competition address in CompetitionFactory
OrganizerCancel competition, advance status (limited)Recorded at competition creation
ParticipantCreate teams, claim prizesOpen, with on-chain validation

8.3 Pause Mechanism

Every contract implements OpenZeppelin's Pausable. When paused, all state-modifying external functions that carry the whenNotPaused modifier revert immediately. Read functions remain available. Pausing is an emergency measure intended for use during security incidents, not routine operations. Unpause requires an explicit owner transaction.

8.4 Integer Overflow / Underflow

All contracts target Solidity 0.8.20, which has checked arithmetic by default. Integer overflow and underflow cause automatic reverts. No unchecked blocks are used in value-handling paths.

8.5 ERC-20 Safety

USDT transfers use low-level .call() with return-value checks rather than the standard IERC20.transfer() interface, to handle non-standard ERC-20 tokens (such as USDT on Ethereum mainnet) that return no boolean. The check pattern is:

(bool success, bytes memory data) = token.call(
    abi.encodeWithSignature("transfer(address,uint256)", recipient, amount)
);
require(success && (data.length == 0 || abi.decode(data, (bool))), "Transfer failed");

8.6 Oracle Trust Assumptions

The oracle is a privileged role. A malicious or compromised oracle could submit incorrect scores, favoring certain teams. Mitigations include:

  • Oracle address is set per-competition; a compromised oracle affects only future competitions unless the owner revokes it.
  • The 90-day dispute window gives participants time to independently verify scores before prizes are disbursed.
  • For high-value competitions, a multisig oracle (requiring k-of-n signatures to submit scores) is recommended.
  • A future upgrade will add an on-chain signature verification scheme for oracle data.

8.7 Audit Status

Pre-Audit Notice: FantasyChain v1.0.0 has not undergone a formal third-party security audit. Operators should commission an independent audit by a reputable firm before deploying in production with significant prize pools. The contracts are provided as-is under the MIT license.

Recommended audit scope: all six contracts with particular focus on PrizeEscrow (fund custody), ClaimManager (withdrawal logic), and CompetitionFactory (prize pool receipt and cancellation refund).

8.8 Upgrade Path

The contracts in v1.0 are not upgradeable proxies. Upgrades require deploying new contract instances and migrating state. For incremental changes, individual contracts (e.g., ScoringManager, ClaimManager) can be redeployed and their addresses updated via admin setter functions on the contracts that reference them (e.g., TeamManager.setCompetitionFactory()). A future release will introduce a proxy-based upgrade pattern with a timelock.

9. Tokenomics & Fee Structure

9.1 Fee Overview

FantasyChain does not have a native protocol token. The protocol earns revenue through a single mechanism: a basis-point fee on participant entry fees. The fee is expressed in basis points and stored in CompetitionFactory.platformFeeBps.

ParameterDefaultRangeWho Controls
Platform fee250 bps (2.5%)0 – 1000 bps (0 – 10%)Contract owner

Consider a competition with a 10 BOT entry fee and 50 participants:

Net to prize pool487.5 BOT → PrizeEscrow

9.3 Free Competitions

Organizers may set entryFee = 0 to create free-to-enter competitions. The prize pool in this case consists solely of the organizer's upfront contribution. No platform fee is collected from free competitions (there is no fee base to calculate from). The protocol earns revenue only from paid competitions.

9.4 Native vs. ERC-20 Competitions

Both native BOT and USDT (or any ERC-20) prizes are supported. The currency is set at competition creation time and cannot be changed. Entry fees must be denominated in the same currency as the prize pool. USDT-denominated competitions require participants to approve() the TeamManager contract before calling createTeam().

9.5 Gas Efficiency

Key gas optimizations in the protocol:

  • Swap-and-pop pattern in PlayerRegistry for O(1) player removal from competition pools.
  • Batch player registration processes up to 100 athletes per transaction.
  • Paginated listCompetitions() avoids unbounded loops in view functions.
  • Scores submitted as an array in a single transaction rather than one-by-one.
  • Solidity optimizer enabled with 200 runs (balanced between deploy cost and call cost).

10.2 Deployment Order

Contracts must be deployed in dependency order because later contracts require the addresses of earlier ones as constructor arguments:

  1. PlayerRegistry — no dependencies.
  2. CompetitionFactory — requires feeRecipient and platformFeeBps.
  3. TeamManager — requires CompetitionFactory and PlayerRegistry addresses.
  4. ScoringManager — requires CompetitionFactory and TeamManager addresses.
  5. PrizeEscrow — requires CompetitionFactory and ScoringManager addresses.
  6. ClaimManager — requires PrizeEscrow and CompetitionFactory addresses.

10.3 Post-Deployment Setup

After all contracts are deployed, perform these administrative steps before opening the platform to users:

  1. Authorize the oracle wallet in CompetitionFactory: setOracleAuthorization(oracleAddress, true).
  2. If using a hot wallet for player registration, authorize it as a registrar: PlayerRegistry.setRegistrar(hotWallet, true).
  3. Update the frontend index.html CONFIG block with all deployed contract addresses.
  4. Conduct a full smoke test: register players, create a competition, submit teams, submit scores, finalize, claim.

10.4 Frontend Integration

The included index.html is a single-file frontend that uses ethers.js (v5 or v6) via CDN and MetaMask for wallet connection. It reads contract ABIs inline and communicates directly with the BOT Chain RPC. To integrate with the deployed contracts:

  1. Update the CONFIG.contracts object in index.html with deployed addresses.
  2. Ensure the ABI JSON embedded in the frontend matches the compiled artifacts in artifacts/contracts/.
  3. Host index.html on any static hosting provider (IPFS, Vercel, Cloudflare Pages).

10.5 Oracle Integration

The oracle is an off-chain service (a Node.js or Python script) that:

  1. Monitors sports data APIs for event completion.
  2. Applies the scoring rules JSON to compute each registered team's score.
  3. Calls CompetitionFactory.updateStatus(id, SCORING) to signal scoring is in progress.
  4. Calls ScoringManager.submitScores(competitionId, teamIds, scores) with the computed scores.
  5. Calls CompetitionFactory.updateStatus(id, FINALIZED) to open prize claims.
  6. Calls PrizeEscrow.finalizeAndAllocate(competitionId) to trigger prize computation.

The oracle wallet must be funded with BOT for gas on all these transactions.

12. Glossary

Basis Points (bps)
A unit equal to 1/100th of a percentage point. 100 bps = 1%. Used to express the platform fee (default: 250 bps = 2.5%).
BOT Chain
A custom EVM-compatible blockchain with chain ID 677 and native currency BOT. FantasyChain is deployed on this network.
Claim Window
The period (default: 90 days from finalization) during which winners may call ClaimManager.claim(). Unclaimed prizes after this window may be swept by the owner.
ClaimManager
The contract through which winners withdraw their allocated prizes, implementing the pull-payment pattern.
Competition
A single fantasy sports event created by an organizer. A competition has a sport type, entry fee, submission deadline, prize pool, and lifecycle status.
CompetitionFactory
The contract that creates and tracks all competitions. Holds the organizer's initial prize pool until finalization or cancellation.
Entry Fee
The amount a participant must pay (in native BOT or USDT) to submit a team to a competition. May be zero for free competitions.
Finalization
The transition of a competition to FINALIZED status, after which prize allocations are computed and claims are opened.
keccak256
The Ethereum-standard cryptographic hash function. Used to hash scoring rules JSON for on-chain commitment.
Non-custodial
A system design where user funds are held in smart contracts with predetermined withdrawal rules, rather than in operator-controlled wallets.
OpenZeppelin
An open-source library of audited Solidity smart contract components. FantasyChain uses Ownable, Pausable, and ReentrancyGuard from OpenZeppelin Contracts v5.
Oracle
An authorized off-chain service that submits real-world athletic performance data (scores) to ScoringManager. Designated per competition at creation time.
PlayerRegistry
The contract that maintains a global registry of real-world athletes eligible for selection in fantasy competitions.
PrizeEscrow
The contract that custodies prize pool funds, computes winner allocations upon finalization, and signals ClaimManager to open withdrawals.
Pull Payment
A payment pattern where recipients actively call a function to withdraw their funds, rather than having funds pushed to them automatically. Mitigates reentrancy and failed-transfer risks.
Registrar
An address authorized to add, update, and remove players in PlayerRegistry. Separate from the contract owner, allowing a hot wallet to manage player data.
ScoringManager
The contract that stores oracle-submitted scores for each team in each competition, and provides ranked results to PrizeEscrow.
Scoring Rules Hash
